How to make any song a ringtone on iPhone

Updated: October 2026 · Works on iOS 26 and iOS 27

Until recently, making a ringtone from a song on iPhone meant GarageBand or a computer and about ten steps. Since iOS 26, Apple added Use as Ringtone, and now it takes under a minute. Here are three ways, from easiest to hardest.

Method 1: SnipRing, nothing to install (fastest)

  1. Open snipring.com in Safari and tap Choose a song.
  2. SnipRing finds the chorus for you. Change the start and length if you like.
  3. Tap Create ringtone, then Set as ringtone.
  4. In the share sheet, scroll down and choose Use as Ringtone. Done.

Your song is never uploaded: everything happens on your phone.

Method 2: a short file you already have

If you already have a clip under 30 seconds as an MP3 or M4A:

  1. Open the Files app and find the file.
  2. Long-press it → Share.
  3. Scroll down and choose Use as Ringtone.

If the song is longer, you need to trim it first. That's exactly what Method 1 is for.

Method 3: GarageBand, for older iOS versions

  1. In GarageBand, create a new Audio Recorder project and switch to Tracks view.
  2. Open the Loop Browser → Files tab, and drag the song onto the track.
  3. Trim it to the part you want, up to 30 seconds.
  4. In My Songs, long-press the project → Share → Ringtone → Export.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a song from Apple Music or Spotify as a ringtone?

No. Streaming songs are copy-protected (DRM), so they can't be trimmed. You need a file you own, such as an MP3 or M4A you bought or recorded.

How long can an iPhone ringtone be?

To set a ringtone directly on iPhone, the file must be shorter than 30 seconds. SnipRing caps it at 29.5 seconds to stay safely within that.

Do I need GarageBand or a computer?

Not on iOS 26 or later. Use as Ringtone in the share sheet sets the file directly.

How do I set a different ringtone for one contact?

In Contacts, open the contact → Edit → Ringtone, and pick your new ringtone.

How do I check my iOS version?

Settings → General → About. Your version is on the "iOS Version" line.
